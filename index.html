The work I do

I work on programs where the stakes are real and the constraints aren’t going away. Legacy systems, regulated environments, complex stakeholder landscapes, non-negotiable timelines. Much of it multi-year, with delivery partners, steering committees, and procurement somewhere on the critical path.

I’ve spent twenty years doing this work – across security, electoral, regulatory, foreign affairs, and enterprise environments – and the most valuable thing a senior practitioner brings isn’t a methodology. It’s judgement.

I work across UX and service design, and increasingly in business analysis and discovery. The job title matters less to me than the outcome.

How I think

01Start with what can’t change

Procurement timelines, security classifications, legacy infrastructure, legislative constraints – these aren’t obstacles, they’re the actual brief. I map the immovables first and design within them, not around them.

↓
02Move alignment from conversation to record

Government programs have stakeholders with competing agendas and different tolerances for ambiguity. I use structured workshops, documented decision logs, and evidence to create alignment that holds – not just in the room, but for six months later when the room has changed.

↓
03Leave things in better shape than you found them

Contractors leave. What matters is what they leave behind. I build artefacts, design systems, and documented patterns that your staff can own and extend without me.
